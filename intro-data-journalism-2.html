Reflection

Perhaps the hardest part of this assignment was finding a dataset interesting and robust enough to spend hours pouring over. The richness and complexity of the Public Plans Data (with over 260 columns and 2700 rows) made for such a dataset —   fun, and sometimes frustrating, entry into a topic I knew very little about. The attached Excel file (also linked at the end of the article) shows some of the steps I took to clean, organise, and make sense of the data. All columns and rows marked in brown represent my own calculations and analyses, which ultimately went into the piece’s Tableau visualisations. The most complicated step involved combing data from each state’s plans into single entries by state and by year. To compute the data in this way, I used a series of SUMIFS statements to selectively add cells.

To verify the trends and important pieces of data I was identifying, I read a number of reports by think tanks, advocacy groups, and market analysts. I then contacted various researchers to comment on US pensions. Their input added depth and insight to my research. Ultimately, the process models the combination of qualitatative and quantitive reporting I’m looking to do in my career.
